ADaPT: Automatic and Deliberate Processes for Trustworthy Robots

Abstract: Trustworthiness is one of the prerequisites to deploy an artificial system in society. Despite recent advancements in computational paradigms, inexpensive hardware, and advancements in robotics, the current robot systems --especially humanoid robots-- present non-negligible limitations to be a trustworthy interaction partner regarding their social and assistive capabilities in performing joint tasks that require safe interactions. To endow humanoid robots with authentic social and assistive skills, this project offers transparent trust models to achieve heterogeneous interactions with humans (or even robots) in lifelong learning settings. To this end, this proposal aims at providing computational models of robot trust by developing automatic (fast and emotional) and deliberate (slow and effortful) processes on humanoid robots.
Overall, the outcomes of this project could be transferred into various applications that require interactions between heterogeneous agents: robot companion for elderly care, language tutoring by cognitive robots, sterilizing the hospital buildings with multi-robot systems, to mention a few. Additionally, the computational models in this research will lead to a better understanding of trust dynamics so that a humanoid robot can continuously acquire skills to perform multiple interactive tasks.
The outgoing phase of this project will be carried out in the Symbiotic Intelligent Systems Research Center (SISReC) at Osaka University, Japan. The returning phase will be at Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin, Adaptive Systems Group, Germany. The humanoid robots --including Torobo, Affetto, Pepper, and Nao-- will be the implementation platforms of this project to show the proposed trust models can be deployed in social and assistive interaction settings to perform real-world tasks
