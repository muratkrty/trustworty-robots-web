![ ](./photos/Sabanovic.jpeg width="210px" align=left) [Selma Šabanović](https://homes.luddy.indiana.edu/selmas/bio.html) is a Professor of Informatics and Cognitive Science at Indiana University Bloomington, where she directs the R-House HRI Lab. Her work combines the social studies of computing with research on human-robot interaction and social robot design. She particularly explores the design, use, and consequences of socially interactive and assistive robots in diverse social and cultural contexts, including various countries, homes, schools, and healthcare environments. Selma currently serves as the Editor in Chief of the ACM Transactions on Human-Robot Interaction. **Title:** Collaborative design methods for trustworthy HRI **Abstract:** The field of HRI has grown to incorporate an increasing set of disciplines, including robotics, psychology, design, and ethics, among others. As robots make their way into everyday use contexts, it is important for HRI to include more diverse groups of domain experts, potential users, and stakeholders in the design of robots. Such inclusion may not only improve the functional fit of robots for their context of use, but also their appropriateness to the values and goals of the communities that will use and be affected by robots. In this talk, I will discuss the use of collaborative and participatory design methods to broaden the inclusion of diverse perspectives in the development, deployment, and design of robots for everyday use. The talk will include examples from community-centered design, design for wellbeing of older adults, and design with children to support their interpretations of human rights.
